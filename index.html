The problem

Business operations should not depend on blind spots.

Small-business workflows often depend on disconnected tools, repetitive manual work, and automation that is difficult to monitor, secure, or recover after failure.

ABAP’s approach

One Docker-based system brings records, automation, integrations, AI services, monitoring, backups, and recovery procedures together—so practical workflows can be built and operated with their supporting controls in view.

Security & recovery

Designed around controls that remain useful when systems fail.

HTTPS, role-based access, server-side secrets, signed integrations, monitoring and recovery procedures support the completed portfolio release.

  1. 01
    Secrets stay outside source control

    Secrets are kept separate from the codebase.

  2. 02
    Signed external webhook requests

    External webhook integrations use HMAC signatures.

  3. 03
    Least-privilege deployment design

    Deployment follows a least-privilege principle.

  4. 04
    Automated encrypted backups

    Scheduled PostgreSQL and n8n backups use encrypted private storage. The corrected scheduled n8n backup completed successfully on November 5, 2026.

  5. 05
    Documented and tested recovery

    Isolated PostgreSQL restoration and recovered-application startup passed health/readiness checks. Separate n8n recovery drills verified SQLite integrity and credential decryption. This is recovery evidence, not a claim of zero data-loss risk.

  6. 06
    Separate public and authenticated AI

    The public chatbot uses reviewed public knowledge. The authenticated Advanced AI Assistant uses only synthetic/public demonstrations; cloud models do not depend on an owner-operated local AI server.
